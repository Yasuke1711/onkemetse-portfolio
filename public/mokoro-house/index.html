01THE LANDSCAPE

A place where
the water
has a rhythm.

In the north-west of Botswana, a river travels a long way to meet a desert. What it leaves behind is not quite land, not quite water, and never quite the same twice.

The Okavango is an inland delta: its waters spread into permanent marshes and seasonally flooded plains rather than flowing to the sea. Its annual flood arrives during Botswana's dry season, bringing an extraordinary change of life to the landscape.

A different kind of season

In the Delta,
the dry season
can bring water.

The flood pulse is part of what makes this place rare. It changes the channels, the grasses and the movement of wildlife, all on the Delta's own timetable.

02THE YEAR IN THE DELTA

A living calendar

Four moods.
One moving world.

The Delta does not follow a simple wet-season / dry-season script. Rainfall, incoming floodwater and local conditions overlap, and the pattern shifts from year to year.

NOVEMBER – MARCH

The land turns green.

Summer rains refresh the wider landscape. The air grows heavy, grasses respond and the Delta's local water levels shift with the weather.

A season of changing skies and fresh growth.

Season descriptions are a broad guide, not a forecast. Flood extent, access and wildlife activity vary with conditions each year.

05A THOUGHTFUL FOOTPRINT

Belong to
the place.
Not over it.

The strongest hospitality concept begins by understanding what should be left untouched.

For Mokoro House, the design ambition is a light visual and physical footprint: architecture that works with shade and airflow, careful thinking about water and energy, and meaningful opportunities for local knowledge and livelihoods.

These are aspirations for an imagined property, not claims about an existing lodge, conservation programme or community partnership.
